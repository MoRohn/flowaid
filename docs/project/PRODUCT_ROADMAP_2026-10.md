# FlowAId product roadmap — October 2026

An evidence-based plan to make every existing capability more dependable, easier to use and more
capable, executed in the same pass that wrote it. Each item names its evidence, the user problem,
the scope, its acceptance check and its status. Status is one of **pending**, **in progress**,
**verified** (acceptance has evidence, listed under [Verification log](#verification-log)),
**blocked** or **deferred** (with the reason).

Baseline: `main` at `cd266b1` (0.9.0 plus the launcher fix). Work lands on the local branch
`roadmap/2026-10`.

## How the evidence was gathered

- **Baseline checks (before any edit):** `pnpm check` green in 42 s (152 turbo tasks); 5,895 tests
  pass without PostgreSQL (web 474, ui 957, workflow-core 2,698, compiler 204, nodes-core 225,
  providers 143, …). The PostgreSQL suites run separately against `flowaid-pgtest`.
- **Running application:** `pnpm start --port 3100 --api-port 3101` against the local database, a
  scratch workspace `roadmap-audit` for anything that changes data, and the real saved data in
  `acme-ops`, `biz-flows` and `default` (read only).
- **Five audit lenses**, each walking its journeys in the browser (1440×900 and 390×844; keyboard
  and pointer) and tracing them through the code: (1) workflows and the builder; (2) runs, human
  tasks and the Overview; (3) agents, templates, knowledge and evaluations; (4) triggers,
  integrations, credentials and settings; (5) the app shell, accessibility, responsiveness and
  performance. No paid generation-model calls were made; about a dozen TypeSafe decision calls were.
- **Performance baseline** (dev server, medians of 3 warm loads; production first-load JS from
  `next build`):

## Roadmap

Priorities: **P0** broken journey or data at risk · **P1** major obstacle or incomplete core
capability · **P2** notable usability or correctness gap · **P3** polish. Items are grouped into
increments in dependency order; shared fixes come before the screens that use them.
